Certifications & Standards

What the Numbers on a Spec Sheet Actually Mean

Window and door performance is described with a handful of recurring terms. Here's what they mean, and where our product lines currently stand.

A note on what's below: the specifications on this page describe the manufacturer engineering behind the uPVC, aluminum, and door systems we install — they are supplier documentation, not certifications held directly by Elegant Windows & Doors. If we hold additional company-level certifications, licensing details, or warranty terms, we'll list them here directly rather than folding them into product specifications.

Reading the Numbers

Three terms worth knowing

R-Value

A measure of how well a material resists heat flow. Higher R-value means better insulation. It's the rating our uPVC supplier publishes for each series.

U-Factor

The inverse idea, used industry-wide by the National Fenestration Rating Council (NFRC): it measures the rate of heat loss through a whole window assembly. Lower U-factor means less heat escapes.

Chambers

The internal air pockets inside a uPVC frame. More chambers generally mean more dead-air insulation and a more rigid frame.

Engineering & Testing

What AAMA and NFRC testing actually checks

Our supplier's profiles are engineered to pass strict U.S. testing metrics in three categories. These are standardized industry test categories, not marketing claims:

Air Infiltration

How much outside air leaks through the assembly under pressure — the basis of a window's draft resistance.

Water Deflection

How well the frame and glazing shed wind-driven rain without letting water past the seals.

Wind Load

The structural pressure a window or door can withstand before deflecting beyond safe limits.

Glass

Low-E, Warm Edge, Argon

Glazing units use Low-E coatings with warm-edge spacer bars and argon gas fill. The Series 80 profile accommodates triple-glazed insulated glass units up to 2-1/8" (54mm) thick.

Finishes

50+ Woodgrain & Color Options

Over 50 architectural woodgrain laminations and durable polymer foil color swatches are available across the uPVC range, with AAMA powder coating and anodized finishes on aluminum systems.
